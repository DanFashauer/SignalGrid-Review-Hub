Cut these on sight:

- **Throat-clearing openings** — "In today's fast-paced world", "It's important to
  note that", "Let's dive in", restating the question before answering it.
- **The tricolon reflex** — everything arriving in threes because three sounds
  complete, whether or not there are three things.
- **"Not just X, but Y"** and its family: "isn't merely", "goes beyond", "more than
  just". Say the thing.
- **Hollow intensifiers** — crucial, vital, essential, robust, seamless, powerful,
  comprehensive, cutting-edge, game-changing, leverage, delve, tapestry, testament.
- **Symmetrical padding** — every paragraph the same length, every section the same
  shape, bullets padded to match each other.
- **Summary that repeats rather than concludes** — a final paragraph that says what
  was just said.
- **Hedging stacked on hedging** — "it may potentially be possible that".
- **Em-dash overuse**, and rhetorical questions used as transitions.
- **Announcing structure** — "First, let's examine…", "Now that we've covered…".

Rules that outrank the list:
- **Do not change a claim to improve the prose.** If a sentence is clumsy because
  it is being precise, leave it clumsy. Flag it instead.
- **Do not flatten specificity.** Numbers, names, caveats and exceptions stay.
- **Shorter is usually right, but not always.** Do not cut an explanation that was
  doing work.

Return the cleaned draft, then a short list of what you cut and anything you left
in on purpose with the reason.
