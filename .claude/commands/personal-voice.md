**If you do not have enough of my writing to work from, ask for a sample before you
start.** Two or three paragraphs I actually wrote is enough. Guessing at someone's
voice produces a caricature, and a caricature is worse than the neutral draft.

Once you have the sample, work from what is observably true in it:

- **Sentence length and rhythm** — do I run long and cumulative, or short and flat?
  Do I use fragments? Where do I break?
- **Vocabulary level and register** — the words I actually reach for, including the
  plain ones I use where someone else would use a fancy one.
- **How I open and close** — most people have habits here they do not notice.
- **What I never do** — the constructions absent from my writing. This shapes voice
  as much as what is present.
- **Punctuation habits** — dashes, semicolons, parentheticals, exclamation marks.

Then return:

1. **The retuned draft.**
2. **The three or four specific features of my voice you used**, quoting the
   evidence from my sample for each.
3. **Anything in the original you kept unchanged because changing it would have
   cost accuracy** — voice never outranks being correct.

Do not caricature. If my sample shows one striking habit, use it about as often as
I do, not on every line.
