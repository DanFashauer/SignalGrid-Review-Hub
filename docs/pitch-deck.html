02 / 11
The problem

Frontline work runs on shared, roaming devices — and no one decides the risky moment.

A nurse pulls a controlled med on a ward tablet three clinicians used this hour. A picker overrides a safety hold. A NOC engineer pushes config to a core switch. Login told you who badged in — not who's holding it now, whether the device is in trusted custody, or whether this specific high-risk action should fire.

Today that decision is stitched across an IdP, a badge system, a UEM, an EDR and an ITSM — inconsistently, per app, with no single verdict and no single audit trail.

03 / 11
The insight

Identity is the wrong layer for a per-action decision.

Who logged in ≠ whether this action, on this device, in this moment, should proceed. That's a live decision — fusing presence, device custody, security baseline and workflow risk — and nothing owns it.

The right place to decide is above the login, at the moment of the action — vendor-neutral, so it works across the whole mixed stack.

04 / 11
The solution

One verdict, rendered invisibly inside the host app.

SignalGrid returns one of four outcomes for the action — and the app just acts on it. The end user never opens a "SignalGrid" app.

ALLOW · runs, invisible STEP-UP · one native gesture RESTRICT · low-risk only DENY · held + logged

Fail-closed by design: unknown or stale evidence raises assurance, never lowers it. At Limited GA the gateway runs in shadow mode — it returns step_up and the host app’s own native authenticator answers it. SignalGrid enforces nothing on the device itself.

06 / 11
How it works

A vendor-neutral access-decision service that sits on top of your stack.

device compliance + how current that answer is + the device's present authority → one verdict · one audit trail
Consumes, doesn't replace

Takes your device-management evidence as input — it doesn't replace the login, and it holds no write route back to any source system.

Custody — a design target, not shipping

Whether a shared device is docked, in-hand, or tampered is proven in the public repository and deferred: it is not evaluated at Limited GA, and no verdict today depends on it.

One plane, mixed fleet

A single allow/step-up/restrict/deny across every app and vendor, with one audit trail.

Deterministic + fail-closed

Unknown or ambiguous input never grants access. The core is pure and provable.

07 / 11
Why now

The per-action gap is widening — and the platform is moving our way.

  • Mixed fleets are the norm. Shared and BYO devices across many vendors, with no one place fusing the per-action decision.
  • Regulatory + audit pressure on high-risk frontline actions keeps rising, but the audit trail stays stitched-together.
  • OS-level signals are getting richer (device management, binary-control, posture) — more inputs for exactly this kind of fused verdict.

Data center / NOC

09 / 11
Business model (design target)

Software decision layer — with an optional hardware trust anchor.

Core — SaaS

Per-device / per-seat subscription for the decision layer and audit plane. Lands as an overlay, no rip-and-replace.

Hardware — later

An embedded trust anchor in the dock / charging bay (custody + tamper at the source). Partner-built hardware, SignalGrid software.

Software first to prove the wedge and land design partners; the hardware anchor deepens the moat and the custody signal once funded.
