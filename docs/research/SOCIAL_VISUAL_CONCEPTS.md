These concepts are for static social graphics or simple carousel frames. Keep them minimal, enterprise-grade, and public-safe.

## Visual style

- Warm charcoal or near-black background.
- Off-white typography.
- Muted teal accent lines or nodes.
- Minimal enterprise layout with generous spacing.
- Simple diagrams over decorative illustration.
- No shields, padlocks, cyberpunk styling, neon gradients, mascots, clutter, threat imagery, or fake product screenshots.

## Concept 1: Fragmented trust signals

Show six separate cards or nodes:

- Identity.
- Device posture.
- Custody.
- Network context.
- Workflow ownership.
- Audit evidence.

Place the question in the center or bottom: "What should happen next?"

Public-safe note: do not use real vendor logos, customer names, tenant IDs, or production screenshots.

## Concept 2: Identity + device + custody + workflow

Use a simple equation-like layout:

Identity + Device + Custody + Workflow + Risk Context → Auditable decision

Add a small caption: "Access-decision orchestration for shared, mobile, and frontline environments." (This line set the retired label until 2026-09-06.)

## Concept 3: "What should happen next?" decision flow

Create a vertical flow:

Signal enters → Context normalized → Decision evaluated → Approval gate checked → Owner routed → Evidence recorded → Verification expected

Use muted teal for the decision and approval-gate steps.

## Concept 4: "Shared device is a session, not an asset"

Large headline: "A shared device is not just an asset."

Supporting labels around a single device outline:

- Session.
- Identity.
- Custody.
- Workflow.
- Location.
- Risk.

Footer: "The next decision needs context."

## Concept 5: SignalGrid above systems of record

Show source-system layers at the bottom:

IAM / MDM-UEM / Readers-Lockers / Network / ITSM / SIEM

Place "SignalGrid access-decision orchestration" above them as a neutral orchestration layer. Add arrows upward for signals and downward for approval-required routes or evidence expectations, not direct production actions.

Public-safe note: label all systems generically and avoid implying current integrations, partnerships, or replacement.
